We use eleven waves of the LISS panel in the Netherlands to study how social and emotional loneliness relate to electoral participation, net of objective social position and objective social integration. Lower self-reported voting propensity goes with social loneliness, the felt lack of a wider community network. Emotional loneliness has no association of its own once social loneliness is accounted for.

The gap is concentrated among citizens who are chronically high in social loneliness. Changes in a person's loneliness over time show no association with voting. The link is therefore dispositional, consistent with weaker voting habits among the chronically lonely.

The gap is much narrower among citizens with more education, more political interest and a party attachment. Loneliness costs the most participation among the citizens with the fewest civic resources.
